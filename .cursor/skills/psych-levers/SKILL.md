# Psych Levers — Five-Lever Persuasion Workflow

Apply five psychological levers in sequence to capture attention, filter the
right audience, create desire, and activate action. Each lever maps to one or
more of the three tensions humans respond to: **Survival** (safety, problems,
solutions), **Identity** (tribe, status, belonging), and **Progress**
(clarity, purpose, transformation).

> Ethics guardrail: persuasion is only as ethical as the person wielding it.
> Use these levers to create genuine value exchange — name real problems,
> mirror real identities, and offer transformations you can actually deliver.
> Do not manufacture threats or promise outcomes you cannot support.

## When to use

- Writing social posts, newsletters, video scripts, or sales pages
- Opening a talk, pitch, or cold outreach message
- Rewriting flat copy that lacks tension or a clear audience
- User invokes `/psych-levers` or asks to "pull psychological levers"

## The three tensions (diagnostic)

Before applying levers, identify which tension(s) the audience is living in:

| Tension | What it is | Signals in audience |
|---------|-----------|---------------------|
| **Survival** | Threat detection, safety, problem-solving | Financial stress, fear of falling behind, "I'm stuck" |
| **Identity** | Tribe, status, belonging, self-concept | "People like me…", ideology, in-group/out-group |
| **Progress** | Meaning, purpose, transformation, clarity | Ready for deep ideas, seeking direction, growth-minded |

People can feel all three, but **stressed audiences stay at Survival** — solve
lower-level problems before pitching transformation. If 90% of the audience
isn't ready for deep insight, lead with Survival/Identity levers first.

## The five levers (apply in order)

```
1. Name the Threat     → Survival tension
2. Mirror the Identity → Identity tension
3. Exclude People      → Identity tension (deepened)
4. Paint Transformation → Progress tension
5. Give the First Step  → Progress tension (activated)
```

Create **tension first**, then offer a **solution**. Never lead with the
solution before the audience feels the problem.

---

### Lever 1 — Name the Threat (Survival)

Before anyone cares about your solution, they must feel the problem.

**Practical step:** Start every piece — newsletter, post, presentation, pitch
— by framing the situation. Name what's wrong, what's at stake, or what
they're missing.

**Problem bank technique:** List 5 problems you've personally experienced →
each one is a content idea better than starting from scratch.

**Examples:**
- "Nobody knows what skill to learn right now."
- "You wake up at 30 and realize you've been living on autopilot."
- "There are people dumber than you making 10x more because they don't think
  about the risk."

**Pair with awareness level:** If the audience is *Unaware*, naming the threat
IS the entire job — make them aware a problem exists. See `/awareness-framing`
for how to calibrate the opening by awareness stage.

---

### Lever 2 — Mirror the Identity (Identity)

Signal "this is for people like you" without saying it in so many words.

**Go-to opener:** `"If you're…"` — then fill in the identity.

**Examples:**
- "If you're lazy."
- "If you're broke."
- "If you're unhappy."
- "If you're a writer."

Each becomes a post hook, video title, or talk opening. Package as:
**Problem + Identity + Solution** in as few words as possible.

---

### Lever 3 — Exclude People (Identity, deepened)

After mirroring who it's *for*, explicitly name who it's *not* for.

Exclusion creates belonging and forces a side. It filters out people who
wouldn't support you anyway.

**Pattern:** `[Mirror identity] → [Draw the line]`

**Examples:**
- "This isn't for people who want to 'try' to get in shape. This is for
  people tired of their own excuses and ready to completely change their
  life."
- "If you're looking for a productivity hack or a morning routine, you're in
  the wrong place."
- "This isn't for people who want a dinky side hustle. We're building a real
  business here."

Do this **after** Lever 2, not before.

---

### Lever 4 — Paint the Transformation (Progress)

Simulate the future. The same neural circuitry fires when imagining an
experience as when having it — give them a felt preview of life after the
change.

**What to paint:**
- Who they become (identity shift)
- What daily life looks like (concrete scene, not abstract benefit)
- What they can now do that they couldn't before

**Examples:**
- "You can distribute the software you build and effectively market any
  product."
- "You navigate life with elegance because you're no longer a slave to your
  mind, but a master of it."

Avoid vague superlatives. Use specific, sensory future-state language.

---

### Lever 5 — Give the First Step (Progress, activated)

Most people don't change because they don't believe they can. Lower the
activation energy with one obvious, beneficial first step.

**Why it works:** The Zeigarnik effect — the brain hates incomplete tasks.
Once someone starts (or even starts *thinking about* starting), tension
wants resolution.

**Examples:**
- Tweet: "Don't overhaul your life overnight — just go to bed an hour earlier."
- Product: make the first action so obvious and beneficial they wonder why
  they didn't do it earlier.

The first step must be **small enough to do today** and **beneficial enough
to feel immediately**.

---

## Workflow

When the user asks you to apply psych levers:

```
1. Diagnose tensions → Which of Survival / Identity / Progress is the audience in?
2. Gather inputs     → Topic, audience, format (post/newsletter/sales page/etc.), goal
3. Apply levers      → Run all 5 in order; skip none unless format is ultra-short (tweet)
4. Audit             → Check each lever landed; flag weak spots
5. Deliver           → Output the copy + a lever-by-lever annotation
```

### Output format

```markdown
## [Format]: [Title/Hook]

[The finished copy]

---

### Lever audit
| Lever | Present? | How it landed |
|-------|----------|---------------|
| 1. Name the Threat | ✓/✗ | [note] |
| 2. Mirror Identity | ✓/✗ | [note] |
| 3. Exclude People | ✓/✗ | [note] |
| 4. Paint Transformation | ✓/✗ | [note] |
| 5. Give the First Step | ✓/✗ | [note] |

**Primary tension(s):** Survival / Identity / Progress
**Suggested tweak:** [one improvement if any lever is weak]
```

### Format-specific guidance

| Format | Lever emphasis |
|--------|---------------|
| **Tweet/X post** | Levers 1+2 in hook; 5 as CTA. Skip 3 unless thread. |
| **Newsletter** | All 5 across intro → body → close. |
| **Sales page** | All 5; Lever 1 pairs with awareness level (see `/awareness-framing`). |
| **Video script** | Lever 2 as title; 1 in first 10 seconds; 4 before offer; 5 at end. |
| **Pitch / talk** | 1 to open, 2+3 to qualify room, 4 for vision, 5 for ask. |

## Short-form template

For quick generation, fill this scaffold:

```
[LEVER 1 — Problem/threat in one sentence]

If you're [LEVER 2 — identity marker]…

[LEVER 3 — optional exclusion: "This isn't for X. This is for Y."]

Imagine [LEVER 4 — specific future scene after transformation].

[LEVER 5 — one small step they can take today].
```

## Red flags — push back

- Solution before problem (Lever 1 missing or buried)
- Generic audience ("everyone" / "people") with no identity mirror
- Transformation is vague ("be successful") with no concrete future scene
- First step is too big ("quit your job and start a business")
- Deep Progress content aimed at a Survival-stressed audience
- Manufactured threats with no real problem behind them
