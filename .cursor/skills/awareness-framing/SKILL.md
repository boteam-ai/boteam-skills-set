# Awareness Framing — Schwartz Five Levels

Match your opening message to how aware the audience already is. The same
product needs different copy at each level — starting with the solution when
they don't know they have a problem is how good offers die quietly.

Source: Eugene Schwartz's five levels of awareness, as applied in Dan Koe's
psychological-lever framework (Lever 1: Name the Threat pairs directly with
awareness level).

## The five levels

| Level | Audience state | Your job in the opening |
|-------|---------------|------------------------|
| **1. Unaware** | Doesn't know they have a problem | **Make them aware** — name the problem they didn't see |
| **2. Problem-aware** | Knows the problem, not the solution | **Name the solution category** — show a path exists |
| **3. Solution-aware** | Knows solutions exist, comparing options | **Differentiate** — why your approach is unique |
| **4. Product-aware** | Knows your offer, hasn't bought | **Prove it** — testimonials, objection handling, risk reversal |
| **5. Most-aware** | Ready to buy, hasn't pulled trigger | **Nudge** — urgency, bonus, final objection, simple CTA |

## Diagnostic workflow

When the user asks you to frame copy by awareness level:

```
1. Identify the offer     → What product/service/message are we selling?
2. Diagnose level         → Ask or infer from context which level fits
3. Pick opening strategy  → Match the table above
4. Draft opening          → Write 2-3 opening variants calibrated to that level
5. Flag level mismatch    → Warn if copy assumes wrong awareness
```

### How to diagnose (when user doesn't specify)

Ask one question if unclear:

> "How aware is your audience of [the problem / solutions like yours / your
> specific offer]?"

Or infer from context:

| Signal | Likely level |
|--------|-------------|
| Cold traffic, broad audience, new category | Unaware or Problem-aware |
| Searching "best X tool" or comparing alternatives | Solution-aware |
| On your email list, visited pricing page | Product-aware |
| In cart, replied "I'm interested", repeat visitor | Most-aware |
| Retargeting ad to past buyers | Most-aware |

If multiple segments exist, **write separate openings per level** or lead with
the lowest-awareness segment (Unaware/Problem-aware) and let deeper segments
skim to proof/CTA.

## Opening strategies by level

### Level 1 — Unaware

**Goal:** Create problem awareness. They don't know anything is wrong yet.

**Techniques:**
- Name a hidden problem in plain language
- Use a relatable scenario that reveals the gap
- Ask a question that surfaces discomfort they haven't articulated

**Pattern:** `[Situation they recognize] → [Reveal the hidden cost]`

**Example:**
> "Nobody knows what skill to learn right now. AI? Coding? Marketing? There
> are so many options that starting feels like choosing the wrong one."

**Do NOT:** Lead with your product name, features, or "buy now."

---

### Level 2 — Problem-aware

**Goal:** Show a solution path exists. They feel the pain; they don't know
what fixes it.

**Techniques:**
- Acknowledge the problem (validate their experience)
- Introduce the solution *category* (not your brand yet)
- Hint at mechanism: "Here's why most approaches fail, and what actually works"

**Pattern:** `[Validate problem] → [Solution category exists] → [Tease mechanism]`

**Example:**
> "You've tried productivity hacks and morning routines. The problem isn't
> discipline — it's that you're solving the wrong level of the problem.
> There's a meta-skill underneath all of them."

**Do NOT:** Jump straight to pricing or feature lists.

---

### Level 3 — Solution-aware

**Goal:** Differentiate your approach from alternatives they're already
considering.

**Techniques:**
- Name the alternatives they know (and their limits)
- State your unique mechanism or angle
- Use comparison: "Unlike X, this does Y because Z"

**Pattern:** `[Acknowledge alternatives] → [Unique mechanism] → [Why it matters for them]`

**Example:**
> "Most persuasion courses teach tactics. This teaches the three tensions
> underneath every tactic — so you can adapt to any platform, any year."

**Do NOT:** Re-explain the problem from scratch (they already know).

---

### Level 4 — Product-aware

**Goal:** Overcome remaining objections with proof.

**Techniques:**
- Testimonials and case studies (specific outcomes, named people)
- Objection handling ("You might think X — here's why that's not an issue")
- Risk reversal (guarantee, free trial, refund policy)
- Show the product in action (demo, screenshot, sample)

**Pattern:** `[Social proof] → [Handle top objection] → [Risk reversal] → [CTA]`

**Example:**
> "2,400 creators used this framework in the last bootcamp. 'I finally
> understand why my posts flopped' — Sarah K. 30-day money-back guarantee."

**Do NOT:** Re-sell the category or re-explain basics.

---

### Level 5 — Most-aware

**Goal:** Give them the final push. They know everything; inertia is the enemy.

**Techniques:**
- Direct CTA with minimal preamble
- Urgency or scarcity (only if real)
- Bonus or incentive for acting now
- Remove friction: one click, one step, one decision

**Pattern:** `[Short reminder of core benefit] → [Urgency/scarcity if real] → [CTA]`

**Example:**
> "Bootcamp starts in 5 days. Enroll before then — link below."

**Do NOT:** Long educational content. They don't need it.

## Common mismatches — flag and fix

| Mistake | Symptom | Fix |
|---------|---------|-----|
| Solution-first to Unaware | Low engagement, "so what?" | Start with problem naming |
| Re-explaining problem to Product-aware | Boring, feels condescending | Jump to proof and objections |
| Feature dump at Problem-aware | Overwhelming, no trust yet | Teach mechanism first |
| No CTA at Most-aware | Lost conversions | Short nudge + direct link |
| One page for all levels | Nobody feels spoken to | Segment or layer: problem → proof → CTA |
